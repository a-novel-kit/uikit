# Shell layout

Every Agora app shares one shell geometry: a collapsible rail beside the page on wide screens, and a header one
control tall, fixed to the top, on narrow ones. `ShellLayout` owns that geometry and the navigation surface the lamp
lights. The app renders its own landmarks, links, labels and account controls into it, and its fullscreen
navigation drawer beside it.

## Rail

The rail stays in view while the page scrolls, and `collapsed` narrows it to one small control. The app's
navigation fills it, inset by `--space-2`, and lays out its own rows. Scroll the examples to try them:

<ResponsiveStoryPair desktop={Stories.ExpandedRail} mobile={Stories.ExpandedRail} meta={Stories} />

<ResponsiveStoryPair desktop={Stories.CollapsedRail} mobile={Stories.CollapsedRail} meta={Stories} />

Under 48rem of width, the rail gives way to the header, fixed to the screen so iOS stretches the page under it past
the top while the header stays in place. The app's header fills it.

## Banner

`banner` holds a notice that stays in view, such as a started `DowntimeBanner`: above the page on wide screens, and
at the bottom on narrow ones, away from where reading starts.

## Usage

```svelte
<script lang="ts">
  import { DowntimeBanner, ShellLayout, SkipLink } from "@a-novel-kit/uikit";
</script>

<SkipLink href="#main-content">Skip to content</SkipLink>
<ShellLayout collapsed={railCollapsed}>
  {#snippet rail()}<aside aria-label="Navigation">…</aside>{/snippet}
  {#snippet header()}<header>…</header>{/snippet}
  {#snippet banner()}<DowntimeBanner {start} {end} started />{/snippet}
  <main id="main-content" tabindex="-1">…</main>
</ShellLayout>
```

Leave `banner` out while there is nothing to show. The page's sticky parts stay clear of the header and banner:
the layout sets `--shell-sticky-top` and, on narrow screens, `--shell-sticky-bottom` to their sizes, which a
`StickyBar` reads.

## Accessibility

The layout adds no landmark, name or focus stop of its own, so the app's landmarks keep their order: rail, header,
banner, then page. In forced colors, the rail and the header keep a border, since the navigation surface is gone.
Reduced motion turns the rail's collapse into a switch.
