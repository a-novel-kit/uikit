Brand is the base and marks interaction. Signal highlights what deserves notice without calling for
action, such as a success or an upcoming change. Pressure marks errors and anything that needs
immediate attention, and, rarer than the brand, a distinction that reports nothing.

## Ink and tint

A hue shows two ways, and they pair. Its ink is the neon itself, for what is drawn thin: text, an icon, a line. Its
tint is a deep step of its ramp, as saturated as that depth allows, for what is filled under its ink: a tag, a chosen
chip, a colored action. The neon never fills behind text: it glared there, and left only black or white to write with.

<div className="pairs">
  <span className="pair pair--brand">Brand</span>
  <span className="pair pair--pressure">Pressure</span>
  <span className="pair pair--signal">Signal</span>
  <span className="pair pair--neutral">Neutral</span>
</div>

| Hue              | Ink                      | Tint                     | Ink on tint |
| ---------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------ | ----------- |
| Brand blue       | `--color-ink-brand`      | `--color-tint-brand`     | 5.7:1       |
| Pressure magenta | `--color-ink-pressure`   | `--color-tint-pressure`  | 4.9:1       |
| Signal amber     | `--color-ink-signal`     | `--color-tint-signal`    | 6.5:1       |
| No hue           | `--color-text-secondary` | `--color-action-neutral` | 7:1 or more |

An ink is its hue's neon, and a tint the third step of its ramp. Without a hue, the pair is secondary text on one
veil.

A pair is lit when it holds the eye: the fill one step up its ramp, and the ink a quarter of the way to white. An
action lights under the pointer and once chosen, to stand out of its own resting look. A label has no resting look to
stand out of, so a badge, a tag and a chosen chip always show lit.

<div className="pairs">
  <span className="pair pair--brand pair--lit">Brand</span>
  <span className="pair pair--pressure pair--lit">Pressure</span>
  <span className="pair pair--signal pair--lit">Signal</span>
  <span className="pair pair--neutral pair--lit">Neutral</span>
</div>

| Hue              | Lit ink                    | Lit tint                       | Ink on tint |
| ---------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ----------- |
| Brand blue       | `--color-ink-brand-lit`    | `--color-tint-brand-lit`       | 6.0:1       |
| Pressure magenta | `--color-ink-pressure-lit` | `--color-tint-pressure-lit`    | 5.0:1       |
| Signal amber     | `--color-ink-signal-lit`   | `--color-tint-signal-lit`      | 6.0:1       |
| No hue           | `--color-text-primary`     | `--color-action-neutral-hover` | 8:1 or more |

The magenta sits darker than the blue and the amber, so its ink takes a quarter of light to read with theirs. Each ink
keeps 5.4:1 or more on a card and in a dialog. A mark that is on, such as a checked box, a switch or the filled part of
a slider, is small: the brand's ink fills it (`--color-control-on`).

## Custom colors

A custom color is one a person gives to an item of their own, such as a tag or a calendar: any opaque color, stored
as a six-digit hex value, or none. It is a family apart from the three hues. It shows only on what a person colored,
never on the interface, which keeps to the three.

| On a colored item         | Token                                                 | Draws                                 |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| A small solid mark        | `--color-mark-custom`                                 | The exact color picked                |
| What is filled under text | `--color-ink-custom` on `--color-tint-custom`         | The color's hue, as a hue's pair does |
| A label                   | `--color-ink-custom-lit` on `--color-tint-custom-lit` | That pair, lit                        |

`customColorStyle` sets these tokens on the item, from its color. The pair takes the color's hue alone, at the
strength every hue's pair has: a pastel, a neon and a dark shade of one hue fill alike, and each ink keeps 4.9:1 or
more on its tint. Text on a custom tint is its ink, or primary or secondary text, never muted. A black, a white or a
gray has no hue, so it keeps its mark and draws the neutral pair. An item without a color draws the neutral pair and
no mark.

[Custom colors](?path=/docs/foundations-custom-colors--documentation) renders the swatches, a range of free colors
and the measured contrast. [Color field](?path=/docs/forms-color-field--documentation) picks one.

A colored action is its hue's pair. The pointer lights it, so the pair keeps
{colorSystem.contrast.minimumNormalText}:1 or more. A chosen action stays lit on a fill one step higher still, which
the pointer never reaches, and a press drops the fill a step under its resting one. The primary action also lightens
toward its top.

Nothing glows: no action, no label, no mark. Light around a deep tint reads as a blur, and color already carries
each one's weight. The rare action that must lead a whole view draws its edge instead
(`Button variant="prominent"`): its ink rises out of its fill, so sharp that it almost reads as a border.

Selected text reads in signal amber on a translucent brand veil (`--color-selection-{text,surface}`).
No other state pairs them, so a selection stands out on every surface. Buttons, tabs and menus are
not selectable.

The pressure magenta also sets apart a distinction that reports nothing, such as a project's genres,
as `Badge tone="accent"` and `ChipGroup tone="accent"` do. A badge and a chosen chip take their tone's pair, and a
neutral badge one veil under secondary text. Each app's lamp takes one of the three hues, never a blend of two
(`--color-lamp-{amber,magenta,blue}`): Account's amber, Studio's magenta.

## Surfaces and lines

Light comes in steps of one veil (`--color-veil`): the lightest neutral, an eighth strong, over whatever lies behind
it. Since a veil is translucent, each role below shows on every surface.

| Role                                            | Token                             | Drawn as                                      |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| A raised card                                   | `--color-surface-island-glass`    | Three quarters of a veil, frosted             |
| What the pointer hovers                         | `--color-surface-glass-hover`     | One more veil                                 |
| A divider between siblings                      | `--color-border-divider`          | One veil, a hairline wide                     |
| An outline around a shape with no fill          | `--color-border-default`          | One veil, `--border-width-outline` wide       |
| A field's well                                  | `--color-control-surface`         | A translucent darkening of its surface        |
| A disabled action                               | `--color-action-disabled-surface` | An inert fill, with no outline and no glow    |
| The rim of a checkbox, a radio button, a switch | `--color-control-border`          | An opaque line that holds around its own well |

An outline takes the color its shape fills with under the pointer, so a ghost card's ring floods the card when it
lights.

A line sits above what it frames. Put a border and its fill on one element, or draw the line as an `outline` when a
layer of its own fills the shape. A ring drawn as a spread shadow, or a child clipped over its parent's border, loses
its antialiasing along every corner.

What holds a control changes how it draws itself. On the canvas nothing else sets a control apart, so a field takes
a ring, every veil outline doubles (`--border-width-outline`), and a disabled action fills with half a veil. An island
carries its controls on its own tone: its fields are bare wells, its outlines hairlines, and its disabled actions take
a field's well. Every uikit island sets this context for its content. An application's own island carries
`data-island`:

```html
<aside data-island>...</aside>
```
