Brand is the base and marks interaction. Signal highlights what deserves notice without calling for
action, such as a success or an upcoming change. Pressure marks errors and anything that needs
immediate attention, and, rarer than the brand, a distinction that reports nothing.

Selected actions keep their peak color and localized glow through interaction. Rest action colors are
derived from steps 9–10 and meet at least {colorSystem.contrast.minimumNormalText}:1 against inverse
text.

Selected text reads in signal amber on a translucent brand veil (`--color-selection-{text,surface}`).
No other state pairs them, so a selection stands out on every surface. Buttons, tabs and menus are
not selectable.

The pressure magenta also sets apart a distinction that reports nothing, such as a project's genres
(`--color-accent-secondary`), as `Badge tone="accent"` and `ChipGroup tone="accent"` do. A badge or a chosen
chip fills with a deep step of its tone's own ramp under light text (`--color-label-*`), so its words read clear
of the glare; every tone keeps 4.5:1. Each app's lamp takes one hue (`--color-lamp-{ember,magenta}`): Account's ember,
Studio's magenta.

## Surfaces and lines

Light comes in steps of one veil (`--color-veil`): the lightest neutral, an eighth strong, over whatever lies behind
it. Since a veil is translucent, each role below shows on every surface.

| Role                                            | Token                                           | Drawn as                                      |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| A raised card                                   | `--color-surface-island-glass`                  | One veil over the canvas, frosted             |
| What the pointer hovers                         | `--color-surface-glass-hover`                   | One more veil                                 |
| A divider between siblings                      | `--color-border-divider`                        | One veil, a hairline wide                     |
| An outline around a shape with no fill          | `--color-border-default`, `--color-border-item` | One veil, `--border-width-outline` wide       |
| A field's well                                  | `--color-control-surface`                       | A translucent darkening of its surface        |
| A disabled action                               | `--color-action-disabled-surface`               | An inert fill, with no outline and no glow    |
| The rim of a checkbox, a radio button, a switch | `--color-control-border`                        | An opaque line that holds around its own well |

An outline takes the color its shape fills with under the pointer, so a ghost card's ring floods the card when it
lights.

A line sits above what it frames. Put a border and its fill on one element, or draw the line as an `outline` when a
layer of its own fills the shape. A ring drawn as a spread shadow, or a child clipped over its parent's border, loses
its antialiasing along every corner.

What holds a control changes how it draws itself. On the canvas nothing else sets a control apart, so a field takes
a ring, every veil outline doubles (`--border-width-outline`), and a disabled action fills with half a veil. An island
carries its controls on its own tone: its fields are bare wells, its outlines hairlines, and its disabled actions take
a field's well. Every uikit island sets this context for its content. An application's own island carries
`data-island`:

```html
<aside data-island>...</aside>
```
